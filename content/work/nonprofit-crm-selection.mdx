    title: An affordable platform wasn't necessarily an affordable decision.

    intro: |
      The nonprofit needed a CRM that could support fundraising operations, fit within a constrained budget, and grow with the organization.

      Both shortlisted platforms offered tiers without monthly platform fees, instead charging per transaction.

      The lower-transaction-fee platform lacked one required feature at its free tier. Accessing that feature through an upgrade was outside the client's budget.

      The competing platform included the necessary near-term capabilities but charged higher transaction fees. It also presented limitations for the nonprofit's more mature fundraising needs over time.

      The apparent choice was between compromising functionality today and accepting higher costs as fundraising activity grew.

      ### A better option through partnership

      Impact Innovations developed a productive relationship with the lower-fee vendor.

      The vendor was interested in supporting organizations like our client, and our firm's ongoing work with the nonprofit offered an opportunity for a longer-term relationship.

      Through that engagement, the vendor enabled the one missing feature without requiring the client to purchase the otherwise unaffordable paid tier.

      The nonprofit could now choose the lower-fee platform without sacrificing its required near-term functionality.
  - eyebrow: 03 / Key Decisions
    title: Change the decision, not the requirements.
    layout: decisions
    children: |
      1. **Evaluate cost across organizational growth**
         A comparison of current prices was insufficient. I modeled transaction costs using configurable fundraising scenarios so that we could understand how the financial difference might develop over time.

         The objective was to avoid solving today's budget problem by creating a larger future expense.
      2. **Preserve the capabilities the nonprofit needed**
         The lower-fee platform was attractive, but its missing feature mattered. Rather than treating that requirement as negotiable, we kept it central to the evaluation.

         The right solution needed to be affordable and usable.
      3. **Treat the vendor relationship as part of the solution**
         The original platform comparison assumed that the available tiers were fixed. Our vendor engagement revealed an alternative: align the client's needs with the vendor's interest in supporting this type of organization.

         That changed which option was feasible.
  - eyebrow: 04 / Results and Reflection

      The nonprofit selected the lower-transaction-fee platform with its required feature enabled.

      Once actual first-year fundraising activity was available, we could compare the selected platform's transaction costs against what the competing fee structure would have charged.

      That analysis confirmed significant first-year savings without requiring the nonprofit to compromise on its immediate functional needs.

      ### What the decision taught me

      A technology decision is not limited to comparing the options presented at the beginning.

      Financial modeling can reveal the consequences of a choice. Relationships and negotiation can sometimes change the choices available.

      The strongest solution emerged from considering all three together: technical fit, economic sustainability, and the interests of the organizations involved.

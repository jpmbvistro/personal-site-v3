  - eyebrow: 03 / Key Decisions
    title: Challenge assumptions. Follow the evidence.
    layout: decisions
    intro: |
      Each decision changed what the team treated as evidence: first for necessary scope, then for correct behavior, and finally for repeatable validation during delivery.
    children: |
      1. **Challenge inherited scope.**
         The backlog was a plan, not evidence of continued need. I built historical traffic views and a reusable endpoint-usage skill to identify calling applications over a year. With Product, technical leads and engineering leadership, I agreed that endpoints without traffic from supported applications could be retired. 37+ planned migrations were removed, and usage verification became part of planning.
      2. **Abandon the unreliable abstraction.**
         Engineers initially pointed to the typed API client as the contract source. It did not reliably represent actual legacy behavior. I stopped relying on static comparison and focused on observable responses and source-level investigation. Response comparisons surfaced discrepancies across 200+ endpoints; subsequent analysis of the legacy implementation identified 75 missed behavioral branches.
      3. **Bring validation into delivery.**
         A one-off investigation was not a repeatable check. Shadow-diff validation was integrated into CI, and engineering leadership prioritized corrective bugs other than negative-path parity issues. Correction and revalidation became the path for handling mismatches—not evidence that every discrepancy or branch had been fixed.
  - eyebrow: 04 / Results and Reflection
    title: Less unnecessary work. Better evidence of correctness.
    layout: prose
    intro: |
      The scope review removed 37+ planned migrations. Behavioral validation surfaced contract discrepancies across 200+ endpoints, and source-level analysis identified 75 missed branches. CI integration made shadow-diff checks repeatable.

      I would establish the source of truth earlier and keep the MVP tighter. I would also involve engineers earlier in investigating discrepancies, so difficult findings become a shared discovery rather than a finished analysis presented to colleagues.
